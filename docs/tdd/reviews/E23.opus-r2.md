## New findings

1. **[blocker] (introduced by the revision) Sections 3.3 and 4.3: the activation payload cannot match E24's
   `SensorEvaluated` type, so every environment switch fails or degrades E24.**
   Lines 209 to 210 publish one `SensorEvaluated` per channel of the new environment at activation. DE23-13 (lines 211
   to 214 and 283) means that at that moment no channel has a reading observed after `activated_at`. So no channel has
   a window end, a reading at E or a received time, and its freshness is `never_reported` (line 218). E24's input type
   requires `window_end: datetime`, `received_at: datetime`, `reading_id: str` and `freshness: Literal["current",
   "stale"]` (E24.md:634 to 639), and T-E23-049 runs E24's contract file against E23's payloads. As written, building
   the payload either fails the activation, the D18 environment switch, or arrives at E24's mapping as an evaluator
   fault (E24.md:964). That marks `rules` degraded and freezes every clear on every switch. The design does not say
   which happens.
   **Required change:** define what activation publishes for a channel with no reading in the new environment. For
   example, publish nothing for it and rely on the first post-activation reading, or publish a typed "no reading yet"
   input agreed with E24 by request. Add an activation case to T-E23-049 and T-E23-069 that runs E24's contract file.

2. **[major] (introduced by the revision) Sections 3.3, 3.4 and 3.7: the DE23-13 cutoff excludes every REPLAY reading
   from every window.**
   Line 283 says: "Only readings observed at or after the active profile's `activated_at` enter a window". A REPLAY
   reading's `observed_at` is its original date (2014 or 2022), always before `activated_at`, so no replay reading
   ever enters a window. The RR-1 CO2 window never evaluates, which breaks golden case 5, D16 and walking skeleton W1.5
   (S8.8). The parenthetical at line 212 tries to make REPLAY an exception through its reference, but 3.7 does not.
   Line 222 has the same `observed_at` cutoff for LIVE and SIM. Line 181 stores pre-init readings as pending and
   "evaluated after init", but they are observed before the init `activated_at` and so can never enter a window.
   **Required change:** for REPLAY, state the cutoff on the hub receive time (readings received at or after
   `activated_at`), consistently in 3.3, 3.4 and 3.7. State what happens to pre-init readings. Add a REPLAY case to
   T-E23-068 and rerun golden case 5 after a mission init.

3. **[major] (introduced by the revision) Sections 3.3 and 3.4: a channel that dies at the switch is never reported
   quiet.**
   With DE23-13, a channel listed for both environments that reported up to the activation, but not after, has no
   reference, so its freshness is `never_reported` (line 218). It never turns `stale`, so no `ChannelFreshnessChanged`
   to `stale` is published, and E24 raises nothing for `never_reported` (E24.md:166). A feed that dies at landing shows
   `no_reading` forever and never raises `sensor_quiet`, the loud failure that section 9 (lines 745 to 746) guarantees.
   **Required change:** for a channel that had readings before the switch, count `activated_at` as its reference in the
   new environment, so it goes stale at `activated_at + stale_after_seconds` without a new reading. Keep
   `never_reported` for channels that have never reported. Test a shared channel that stops at activation.

4. **[major] (introduced by the revision) Section 3.1: `clock_state` depends on a heartbeat the hub cannot receive.**
   `trusted` needs "the channel's adapter sent a heartbeat within `sensors.adapter_heartbeat_max_s` reporting its clock
   synchronized" (line 152), and with no heartbeat the state is `unknown` (line 154). The only adapter-to-hub interface
   is `ingestReadings` (line 558). `AdapterReadingBatch` carries no heartbeat or clock field, and the adapter's
   heartbeat lives on its own `/health` endpoint (line 578). Nothing says the hub polls it or how. As written, every
   LIVE and SIM evaluation is `unknown`. E24 counts dwell evidence only when `clock_state == "trusted"` (E24.md:186),
   so no SIM sensor warning ever clears by its condition, which breaks V56 and the demo acts.
   **Required change:** define the heartbeat path. Either the hub polls each adapter's `/health` with a timeout and
   bound (part of `AdapterHealthSource`, with its hub settings), or a contract field on the batch is requested.
   Define `clock_state` for SIM, whose clock is the generator's. Test that a heartbeat lapse gives `unknown` and a
   healthy SIM feed gives `trusted`.

5. **[major] (introduced by the revision) Section 3.7: the stuck value check misfires or never fires, and borrows a
   number that means something else.**
   Lines 309 to 312 flag a channel as stuck when "every accepted reading in the last `stale_after_seconds` (at least
   two) has the same canonical value". On the demo CO2 channel, interval 300 s and stale time 300 s, at most two
   readings fall in that span. If both ends are included, two equal consecutive readings trigger the check. In the
   golden window, the RR-1 file has 4531 ppm four times in a row from 15:06 to 15:21, which is quantized data, not a
   fault. If the span is half open, two readings never fit and the check never fires. The design also does not say
   whether the span is in data time or in `safe_now`. `stale_after_seconds` is a "team default" freshness bound
   (sensor-catalog example `stale_basis`), not a sourced stuck criterion, so the check takes a number with no
   provenance for this meaning (AGENTS rule 1). It also reuses `insufficient_data`, whose C13 meaning is coverage
   (CONTRACTS.md:696).
   **Required change:** take the stuck criterion (count and span) from R-E23-2's catalog proposal, with a basis. Until
   then, either run it with a stated, sourced team-default count or leave it off and say so on the card. Define the
   time base. Do not overload `insufficient_data` without a request. Test it on the RR-1 golden window, where it must
   not fire.

6. **[minor] (introduced by the revision) Sections 3.4 and 9: the "independent" sweep shares the scheduler with the
   deadlines.**
   The sweep is re-registered at `STATE_SWEEP` and then runs every `sensors.freshness_sweep_s` (lines 237 to 239).
   That is a job in the same `platform_scheduler` that runs the deadlines (S5.10). A stalled or crashed scheduler
   stops both, and section 9 (line 737) still calls it independent. T-E23-019 deletes the deadlines but never stalls
   the scheduler.
   **Required change:** state the common mode. Add a scheduler lag watchdog that marks health degraded and shows it
   where the Habitat screen reads health, or run the sweep on its own timer. Test a stalled scheduler.

7. **[minor] (introduced by the revision) Section 3.8 step 6: at the same instant, the later value is silently left
   out of evaluation.**
   With two different values at one `observed_at` on one channel, "only the earlier one enters windows" and the
   conflict appears only in the later reading's provenance `trace` (lines 342 to 344). If the later value crosses a
   limit, the crossing is never evaluated, and nothing on the card or in health says so. AGENTS.md:216 says to flag
   the conflict.
   **Required change:** surface the conflict in the card's `trace` and in adapter health. Choose the conservative value
   for evaluation, or file the rule as a request, and test it.

8. **[minor] (introduced by the revision) Section 3.3: before init, `recordMissionEvent` returns an undeclared code.**
   Line 182 answers every mission command with `409 INVALID_TRANSITION` before init. `recordMissionEvent` declares only
   `409 DUPLICATE_COMMAND` (openapi.yaml:1804 to 1808; line 614 here).
   **Required change:** use a declared code for that operation, or carry the 409 through the `default` envelope and
   add it to a request, as R-E23-12 does.

9. **[minor] (introduced by the revision) Section 4.2: `DayLabelPort` labels a past instant with today's profile.**
   `inline_label_at(instant)` renders "the active profile's `day_label.inline_template`" (lines 475 to 476). E15, E28
   and E31 label past instants: episode times, due times, practice days. A transit-era instant would read as a sol
   after a switch to Mars.
   **Required change:** render with the profile that was active at `instant`, through `ProfileDayPort.at(view,
   instant)`. Add such a case to T-E23-010.

10. **[minor] (introduced by the revision) Section 5.2: the adapters' demo gate depends on E29.**
    Both CLIs start only if `getMission` reports SIM or REPLAY (line 574), but `getMission` answers `INTERNAL_ERROR`
    whenever E29 is absent or failing (line 633). A failing ground-link module then stops every habitat feed.
    **Required change:** read the mission mode from an operation or check that does not need the ground link, or treat
    that `INTERNAL_ERROR` (which names `ground_link`) as "mode unknown, retry" with a stated bound, and test it.

11. **[minor] (introduced by the revision) Section 3.7: the window scan guard uses the catalog interval, but channels
    override it.**
    The activation guard estimates the scan for `max` and `sum` windows "at the catalog interval" (lines 294 to 295).
    Registry channels may override `expected_interval_seconds` (line 589), and an adapter may report faster than its
    interval. At run time the bound then raises "a typed error and health degraded" (line 802), which switches off the
    limit, the outcome lines 294 to 295 say cannot happen.
    **Required change:** run the guard on the fastest registered channel interval too, and validate the registry
    against it. State the run-time behavior honestly: the status is shown as not evaluated, with the reason.

12. **[minor] (introduced by the revision) Section 5.2: there are two definitions of the demo feeds.**

    **Required change:** agree with E21 on one feed file and one owner (a request to E21 and an S4.3 entry for the new
    fixture folder), and make one consume the other.
